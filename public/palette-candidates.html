Batch Three and Beyond

Sixty new colour-profile candidates spanning art, design, industry, early computing, photography, textile, systems and the everyday — picked against the gaps the shipped sixteen leave, not down a list of nice names.

This page is the reasoning behind the queue. The full list — all 128 rows including the shipped built-ins and the original forty, as a spreadsheet — is palette-candidates.csv in the repository, alongside the ticket that governs how any of them would ship.

What this list is, and what it is not

These are unsigned ideas, exactly as ICE-PROFILES-02 describes its own forty. None has a rule, a membership, or test-image evidence behind it. Nothing here reaches the profile select without going through the same batch process the shipped sixteen did: agent drafts with evidence, owner curates names and membership, only signed batches ship.

Everything below was checked against the twenty-five shipped built-ins and the forty already queued. Where a candidate sits close to something that exists, the overlap is named rather than hidden — the acceptance record judged the gallery on “none named redundant”, and that is the bar a new batch has to clear too.

Picked against gaps
Batch two was chosen against what batch one left uncovered. Same method here: the gallery still has no red, no violet, no achromatic ladder, no all-hue dark, one two-pole shape, and nothing at all from industry.
Eligible universe, not palette
A rule-shaped profile narrows the catalogue; the design’s colour limit then selects from what survives. Hundreds of entries is the healthy shape. Curated means a rule would misdescribe the style.
Names are style, never trademark
Every name below is a technique, material, place or system. None trips the existing guard — but the guard is a fixed list, so it cannot catch a new mark on its own. See the naming section.
Ladders admitted (D204)
These were first held back as tone-mode ramps. The owner ruled otherwise, and the reading was too broad: CREATIVE-01’s warning is about the control shape and the naming, not the membership. Eight ladders are in, and a ladder profile feeding tone mode is the pairing the two features were built for.
The pool leans curated — deliberately mix
Only 14 of the 48 are rule-shaped, against a shipped gallery that is 9 rule to 7 curated. That is the untouched territory being what it is: industry, systems and textile palettes were specified by somebody, so a colour-space band would misdescribe them. Batches drawn from this pool have to reach for the rule-shaped candidates on purpose, which is why the eight below are four and four.

Proposed batch three

4 rule · 4 curated · six gaps closed

The same four-and-four split batch two used. Every one of these closes a named hole in the gallery rather than adding to a region already served, and two of them open territory the gallery has never had.

  • rule Grisaille Near-zero chroma across the full lightness range. The gallery has no thread-based neutral ladder at all — Black & white is two entries and Greys is four generated levels.
  • rule · 2 bands Chiaroscuro Any hue at very low brightness, plus a narrow high-key band. Every dark profile shipped is hue-restricted: Deep sea is cool, Rainforest is green.
  • rule Vermilion and madder The red arc alone, at real chroma. Nothing in the gallery owns red — Autumn leaves begins at hue 10 and is already orange by the time it has any width.
  • rule · 2 bands Teal and orange A warm arc against a cool one. Neon noir is the only two-pole shape shipped, and its poles are magenta and cyan.
  • curated Anodised aluminium Dyed metal: the violet-blue, acid green, gold, red and raw silver. The violet corner is the gallery’s largest unclaimed region.
  • curated Heraldic tinctures Gules, azure, vert, sable, or, argent, purpure. Seven colours defined by a real historical system — and the second thing on this list that hands you purple.
  • curated High-visibility safety Fluoro yellow-green and orange against black and silver. The acid corner is empty; Fluoro spot print is print inks, which is a different animal.
  • curated Transit map lines One entry per hue sextant at maximum separation — colours chosen so they are never confused. Directly useful in a chart app, where legibility is the product.

Art and pigment

10 candidates

The queued forty covers movements. This covers materials — what a given medium physically permits — which is the half that turns into a rule more honestly than a movement does.

CandidateLeanWhat it isFills / check against
Grisaille rule The paint-in-greys underpainting: chroma at or near zero, but the whole lightness range, so it reads as a ladder rather than a pair. Fills the achromatic gap. No thread-based neutral set exists.
Chiaroscuro rule · 2 bands Any hue at very low brightness, plus a narrow high-key highlight band. The gap between the two bands is the style. Fills all-hue dark. Deep sea and Rainforest are both hue-locked.
Vermilion and madder rule The red arc alone, from scarlet through crimson to rose, held at real chroma so it never drifts into brick. Fills red. Autumn leaves starts at hue 10 and is orange in practice.
Fresco lime plaster rule What survives being painted into wet lime: earth hues capped at middling chroma and held bright. Chalky by construction, not by taste. Check against Sepia, which is broader and reaches much darker.
Verdigris and bronze rule Patinated metal: the blue-green copper oxides set against warm unweathered bronze. A narrow arc nothing else touches. Fills the blue-green seam between Rainforest and Deep sea.
Sumi ink wash ladder A warm-black dilution ladder — the same idea as Grisaille one temperature over, and the older of the two by centuries. The warm counterpart to Grisaille’s cool. Ship the pair or pick one.
Cave ochre and charcoal curated Red ochre, yellow ochre, charcoal black, chalk white. Four or five entries: the oldest palette there is, and honestly tiny. The smallest curated set proposed. Tests the low end of the count floor.
Egg tempera and gold ground curated Vermilion, lapis, ochre, verdigris, gold, ivory — the icon painter’s shelf, where every pigment was chosen for permanence first. Opens the medieval and devotional territory the gallery lacks.
Expressionist woodcut curated Black, raw paper white, and one or two unmixed colours cut straight into the block. Crude on purpose. Deliberate counterweight to Ukiyo-e woodblock’s refinement.
Botanical plate curated Hand-coloured engraving on cream: soft leaf greens, madder pinks, ochre, and a sepia line holding it together. Check against Spring meadow — softer and hue-wider than a green rule.

Design and print

8 candidates

Where the colour set was specified by somebody, for a reason. These read as curated because they genuinely are — a designer wrote the list down, and a colour-space rule would drag in every neighbour they rejected.

Brutalist concrete rule Board-marked concrete: near-neutral mids with a distinctly cool cast, plus rust and moss staining where the water runs. Close to Moorland. Only ships if the cool cast is a real separation.
Transit map lines curated One entry per hue sextant at maximum mutual separation — a set built so no two lines are ever confused at a glance. Fills distinguishability. Nothing shipped optimises for telling colours apart.

Constructivist curated Red, black, cream, ochre — the warmer, dirtier cousin of the above, printed on stock that was never white. Overlaps the previous row. Ship at most one of the two.
Ceramic glaze curated Celadon, tenmoku, oxblood, ash, iron. Kiln colours: muted, deep, and unlike anything a dye produces. Adds a muted-but-deep register between Moorland and Deep sea.

Industry and infrastructure

8 candidates

Entirely new territory. Neither the shipped sixteen nor the queued forty contains a single industrial entry, and it is the richest untouched seam: these palettes were specified for function, so their membership is documented fact rather than taste.

CandidateLeanWhat it isFills / check against
High-visibility safety curated Fluoro yellow-green and orange against black and retroreflective silver. Colours chosen to be seen, not liked. Fills acid. Fluoro spot print is print ink, a different set.
Hazard and warning curated The three signage pairs — yellow on black, white on red, white on blue — and nothing else. Overlaps the row above at yellow. Ship at most one.
Machinery and plant curated Plant green-grey, dust yellow, oxide primer red, and black. The colours of things built to be repainted. Muted-but-warm; sits between Moorland and Sepia without being either.

Cockpit instrument curated Dark panel grey-green, amber, white, and warning red. A palette designed to be read in the dark. A dark ground with two hot accents — structurally like Neon noir, tonally nothing like it.
Circuit board curated Solder-mask green, gold pad, black component, tinned silver, and the red and blue of jumper wire. An unusual green — dark, and far more yellow than Rainforest.
Shipping container rule Everything faded by a year at sea: mid chroma across all hues, nothing bright, nothing black. Salt does the narrowing. Check against Moorland; this is brighter and hue-unrestricted.

Early computing

7 candidates

Added on request, and the category needs care in two directions at once. Three of its most obvious entries are already shipped — recording those is the useful half. And every machine that defined a palette also defined a trademark, so all seven are named by mechanism rather than by maker.

CandidateLeanWhat it isFills / check against
Handheld green LCD ladder Four greens and no more — the whole machine’s output, and probably the most recognisable four colours on this page. The clearest ladder in the pool. Judge below limit 8.
Green phosphor ladder Black to a single green, by brightness alone. A terminal had no second hue to offer. Pairs with Amber phosphor — ship both or neither.
Amber phosphor ladder The same shape one hue over, and the one people chose when they had to look at it all day. Pairs with Green phosphor.
Four-colour adapter curated Black, cyan, magenta, white; the alternate mode swaps in red, green and a dark yellow nobody liked. The restriction is the style. Not Retro 16 — the point is what is missing, not what is present.
Composite artefact colour curated The purple, green, blue and orange fringes that appeared where no colour was ever sent — colour as a side effect of the signal. Nothing in the gallery resembles it.
Home micro brights curated Eight hues at two brightness levels. The dim/bright pairing is the whole character — without it this is just the corners. Investigate — may collapse into 1-bit RGB.
Home console curated The washed, low-contrast console palette with no true saturation at either extreme — a television’s idea of colour. Investigate against pool A’s 8-bit arcade.

Heat, light and sky

3 candidates

The one group here defined by shape rather than territory, which is why it carries no colour of its own: three ladders that describe graduated light. All three were on the excluded list until the ladder call.

CandidateLeanWhat it isFills / check against
Foundry heat ladder Black through cherry red, orange and yellow to white — the blackbody curve, which is a ladder that occurs in nature rather than one somebody drew. The most legible ramp in the pool.
Stellar temperature ladder Red through white to blue. A ladder that crosses hue rather than staying on one, and runs the opposite way to every instinct about warm and cool. Tests whether a ladder must be single-hue to read as one.

Photography and film

6 candidates

The strongest rule-shaped seam on the list. A film stock or a grade is a transformation of colour space, so the honest expression really is a band or a pair of bands — and users already recognise these looks without being told the name.

CandidateLeanWhat it isFills / check against
Teal and orange rule · 2 bands A warm skin arc against a cool shadow arc, with the greens and magentas between them left out entirely. Fills the warm/cool pole shape. Neon noir is magenta/cyan.
Cross-processed rule · 2 bands Highlights pushed yellow-green, shadows pushed cyan-blue. The two bands are the whole effect — take one away and it is just a cast. A hue split by brightness, which no shipped profile does.
Instant film rule Milky and warm: low chroma held at mid-to-high brightness, with the hue range skewed off neutral rather than centred on it. Check against Pastels, which is lighter and carries no hue skew.
Sun-bleached print rule Cyan dye fades first, so an old print drifts magenta-yellow. A warm arc at low chroma — the physics of a photograph left in a window. Check against Sepia; this keeps pink, which Sepia has none of.
Bleach bypass rule Chroma stripped almost out, but the blacks kept full. Silver retained on the print, in one sentence. Close to Grisaille and Moorland. Likely redundant — verify before drafting.
Screen-plate colour curated The earliest colour photography: soft orange-red, green and blue-violet dyed grains, laid over a warm haze that never quite clears. Named by process, not by the maker’s mark. See naming.

Textile and dye

7 candidates

Tartan sett curated Bottle green, navy, deep red and black, with white and yellow overchecks carried a single thread wide. Check against Fair Isle — darker, dyed rather than undyed.

The ladder question, and what really is not a profile

Eight of these candidates are single-hue lightness ladders. They were first held back, on the reading that CREATIVE-01’s “the ramp control and a ‘ramp’ profile shape must not collide” ruled them out. That reading was too broad and the owner overruled it: the warning is about the control shape and the naming, not about the membership being forbidden.

The consequence runs the opposite way from a collision. A ladder profile feeding tone mode is the pairing the two features were built for — a narrow ordered ramp as the eligible universe, matched by the tone metric, is gradient-map behaviour reached through two controls that already exist, with no new code. Two things follow for drafting: a ladder is where order is identity bites hardest, so it ships written light-to-dark; and its evidence has to be read at a colour limit below the default eight, because a five-rung ladder selected down to eight is the whole profile. One bonus — queued Sashiko indigo is a ladder and no longer needs re-filing.

Mixing behaviours still belong to dither

Pointillism, ikat’s blurred edges, halftone and moiré weave are all descriptions of how colours combine, not of which colours are eligible. The project has already learned this the hard way — batch one’s evidence could not be reproduced until dithering was included, because the app’s Floyd–Steinberg is what turns an edge into a mix. These are dither ideas wearing palette names, and they are the one group that genuinely does not belong here.

Naming

No name on this list trips the guard in tests/color-profile.test.ts — which has itself been extended as part of this pass. Four notes.

  • Two names were pre-emptively changed. “Screen-plate colour” avoids the maker’s mark on the early colour process, and “Sports pitch” avoids a trademarked road-surface name — the same good-faith failure that turned Risograph print into Fluoro spot print.
  • The guard was extended, because a fixed list cannot catch a new mark. Technicolor sat in the queued forty and passed the guard for two batches. Now added and asserted: technicolor, technicolour, kodachrome, polaroid, formica, perspex, tarmac, astroturf, day-glo, letraset, plus atari, amiga, sega and game boy for the new category.
  • Two words were deliberately left out, and are asserted as passing. spectrum names a home micro but is first an ordinary colour word — guarding it would condemn “Spectral bands”. commodore names a machine and a naval rank, and a maritime palette has every right to it. That is the same lesson that once made the guard match ral inside “Coral reef”: a guard that rejects a legitimate name is worse than none, because the fix looks like renaming the profile.
  • Cultural names need the owner’s explicit call. Kente, Suzani and Sari name living traditions. Fair Isle and Ukiyo-e set the precedent that place and tradition names are acceptable, but these carry more weight; where the cloth and the people have different names, prefer the cloth. The queued forty has the same question hanging over Talavera tile and Pop art (Warhol).
